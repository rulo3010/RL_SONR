# RL_SONR
Estimating the incurred but unreported claims (SONR) reserve in low-frequency, high-severity lines of business presents significant challenges due to data scarcity and high variability. Traditional actuarial methods, such as Chain Ladder, rely on static assumptions selected based on historical analysis and expert judgment, limiting their adaptability to structural changes. This paper proposes a dynamic reserve management approach using reinforcement learning, where an agent sequentially optimizes the selection of actuarial assumptions, such as development factors. The model incorporates a systemic reserve approach by integrating market information and capturing relevant external effects on claims experience. The methodology is evaluated against traditional approaches in terms of accuracy, bias, and stability, with the aim of improving the adaptability and robustness of reserve estimation.\\

\textbf{Keywords:} Reinforcement learning, SONR, Chain Ladder, volatility, risk.
